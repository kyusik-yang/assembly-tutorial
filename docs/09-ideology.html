Ideal Points & Ideological Positioning

W-NOMINATE and DW-NOMINATE ideal points, polarization trends, and spatial voting models for the Korean National Assembly

Overview

Roll-call scaling methods such as W-NOMINATE and DW-NOMINATE (Dynamic Weighted Nominal Three-Step Estimation) place legislators on an ideological scale using their roll-call voting records. Unlike party labels - which shift with frequent mergers and rebrandings in Korean politics - ideal points provide a continuous, member-level measure.

The kna master database (release 0.7.0) includes 940 ideal-point estimates for the 20th-22nd Assembly (2016-present), one per legislator-term, in three series. The default series is per-assembly W-NOMINATE, aligned across terms using the legislators who served in consecutive assemblies. A pooled DW-NOMINATE series is shipped alongside it.

Changed in kna 0.7.0
  • dw_ideal_points_20_22.csv and its columns aligned and coord1D are gone. Despite its name, that file never held DW-NOMINATE output. Use db.ideal_points() or read ideal_points_bridged.csv.
  • party is the party at election, the party whose list or ticket the member was elected on. Earlier files carried the API’s current party, applied to every past vote.
  • party_bloc places 새누리당 and the satellite list parties 미래한국당 and 국민의미래 in the conservative bloc, and the satellite list parties 더불어시민당 and 더불어민주연합 in the liberal bloc.
  • Every row carries a vintage. The default vintage is v20260917, estimated from the votes through 2026-09-17. The 0.6.0 files are kept in data/processed/ideal_points_archive/v0.6.0_legacy/ for reproducing earlier results.

Why roll-call ideal points for Korea?

Korean parties undergo frequent mergers, splits, and name changes. What was the Grand National Party (한나라당) became the Saenuri Party (새누리당), then the Liberty Korea Party (자유한국당), then the United Future Party (미래통합당), and finally the People Power Party (국민의힘) - all within a decade. The progressive bloc has undergone similar rebranding cycles.

Simple party-label-based ideology measures cannot capture:

  • Within-party heterogeneity (moderates vs. hardliners)
  • Cross-party bridges (legislators who vote across party lines)
  • Temporal shifts (has the same party moved left or right?)

Roll-call-based ideal points solve all three. A W-NOMINATE fit of one assembly places its legislators on one scale. Comparing assemblies needs a common scale, which kna builds from bridging legislators - members who serve in more than one term. The bridged series maps each assembly onto the previous one, and pooled DW-NOMINATE estimates the three assemblies together.

Data foundation

Metric Value
Legislator-terms 940 (317 in the 20th, 318 in the 21st, 305 in the 22nd)
Assemblies 20th (2016-20), 21st (2020-24), 22nd (2024-)
Roll call votes 2,557,618 member-level records on 8,610 recorded votes
Contested votes scaled 269 / 331 / 223 (at least 2.5% of those voting yea or nay in the minority)
Bridging legislators 218 in two or more terms (126 in both the 20th and 21st, 151 in both the 21st and 22nd, 68 in all three)
Estimation method W-NOMINATE per assembly via R wnominate, chained bridging, and pooled DW-NOMINATE via R dwnominate
Sign Positive = conservative (보수), negative = liberal (진보), anchored on 추경호 (G152611B)
Vintage v20260917, votes through 2026-09-17

A legislator is scaled with at least 20 contested yea or nay votes. 찬성 counts as yea and 반대 as nay, while 기권 and 불참 are treated as missing. The 22nd Assembly is in session, so its values are provisional. The 16 members seated during 2026 are missing from the vote API and have no 22nd ideal point.

Three series

series= File Column Comparable within an assembly Comparable across assemblies Movement of a legislator
"bridged" (default) ideal_points_bridged.csv bridged_1d Yes Yes Partly
"wnominate" ideal_points_wnominate.csv wnom_1d Yes No No
"dwnominate" ideal_points_dwnominate.csv dwnom_1d Yes Yes No, constant

The bridged series equals per-assembly W-NOMINATE in the 20th. It maps the 21st onto the 20th and the 22nd onto the aligned 21st with a least-squares line fitted on the legislators who served in both, which assumes that these legislators do not move on average. With only three terms, pooled DW-NOMINATE admits one position per legislator for all terms, so changes in its party means across assemblies come entirely from turnover. Section 10 of the kna codebook describes the estimation.

Loading ideal points

Python (via kna)

from kna.data import BillDB

db = BillDB()
ip = db.ideal_points()  # bridged series (default)

print(f"Legislator-terms: {len(ip)}")
print(f"Columns: {list(ip.columns)}")
print(ip.head())

# The other two series
wnom = db.ideal_points(series="wnominate")
dwnom = db.ideal_points(series="dwnominate")
Legislator-terms: 940
Columns: ['member_id', 'member_name', 'party', 'party_bloc', 'term', 'wnom_1d', 'ideal_point', 'vintage']

Key columns:

Column Description
member_id MONA_CD (unique legislator identifier)
member_name Legislator name. Names repeat, so join on member_id
term Assembly number (20, 21, 22)
party Party at election in that assembly, including satellite list parties
party_bloc Broad bloc (conservative, liberal, progressive, rebuilding, centrist, liberal_minor, independent)
ideal_point First dimension of the series. db.ideal_points() renames bridged_1d, wnom_1d or dwnom_1d to this name
wnom_1d Bridged series only. The per-assembly W-NOMINATE value before alignment
vintage v followed by the date of the last vote used

The ideal_point column of the default series is what you should use for analysis. It is:

  • Cross-assembly comparable through bridging, under the assumption that the bridging legislators do not move on average
  • Signed to match political convention, with negative = liberal (진보) and positive = conservative (보수). No sign flip is needed.

R

library(readr)
library(dplyr)

# Path relative to the root of the kna checkout
ip <- read_csv("data/processed/ideal_points_bridged.csv",
               col_types = cols(member_id = col_character())) %>%
  rename(ideal_point = bridged_1d)  # positive = conservative, no flip needed

ip %>%
  group_by(term, party_bloc) %>%
  summarise(n = n(), mean_score = mean(ideal_point), .groups = "drop") %>%
  arrange(term, mean_score)

Polarization analysis

Polarization is measured as the distance between the liberal and conservative bloc means:

\[\text{Polarization}_t = |\bar{x}_{liberal,t} - \bar{x}_{conservative,t}|\]

# party_bloc already groups the parties, including 새누리당 and the
# satellite list parties. A hand-written party list can silently drop
# members, because party is now the party at election.
blocs = ip[ip["party_bloc"].isin(["liberal", "conservative"])]

polar = (
    blocs
    .groupby(["term", "party_bloc"])["ideal_point"]
    .mean()
    .unstack()
)
polar["gap"] = abs(polar["liberal"] - polar["conservative"])
print(polar.round(3))

Expected output:

party_bloc  conservative  liberal    gap
term
20                 0.453   -0.364  0.818
21                 0.456   -0.380  0.836
22                 0.427   -0.382  0.809

In the bridged series the gap is 0.818 in the 20th, 0.836 in the 21st and 0.809 in the 22nd, 1.0% smaller in the 22nd than in the 20th. The three series do not give the same trend, so it is worth computing the gap in each:

for series in ["bridged", "wnominate", "dwnominate"]:
    d = db.ideal_points(series=series)
    d = d[d["party_bloc"].isin(["liberal", "conservative"])]
    means = d.groupby(["term", "party_bloc"])["ideal_point"].mean().unstack()
    gap = (means["liberal"] - means["conservative"]).abs()
    print(f"{series:<11}", gap.round(3).tolist(), f"{gap[22] / gap[20] - 1:+.1%}")
bridged     [0.818, 0.836, 0.809] -1.0%
wnominate   [0.818, 0.931, 1.145] +40.1%
dwnominate  [0.781, 0.825, 0.846] +8.4%

The per-assembly W-NOMINATE gaps are not comparable across assemblies, because each fit fixes its own unit from the configuration it recovers. What a per-assembly fit identifies is the ratio of the distance between the blocs to the dispersion within them. Bridging rescales distance and dispersion by the same factor and leaves that ratio unchanged. The kna codebook therefore recommends reporting within-party dispersion together with the distance, and treating the growth in the per-assembly series as an upper bound (CODEBOOK.md, section 10.4).

Within-party sorting

Polarization can increase through two mechanisms, blocs moving apart (divergence) or members within blocs becoming more homogeneous (sorting). Standard deviations reveal which:

sorting = (
    blocs
    .groupby(["term", "party_bloc"])["ideal_point"]
    .agg(["mean", "std", "count"])
    .round(3)
)
print(sorting)
                    mean    std  count
term party_bloc

Declining standard deviations indicate sorting - fewer moderates within each bloc. The ratio of the gap to the pooled within-bloc standard deviation is 5.18, 7.04 and 10.35 in the 20th, 21st and 22nd in the bridged series, and 7.14, 7.18 and 6.90 in the pooled DW-NOMINATE series.

Using ideal points in regression

As independent variable

Ideal points predict legislative behavior beyond party labels:

library(arrow)
library(fixest)

# Does ideology predict bill passage (with committee fixed effects)?
bills <- read_parquet("data/processed/master_bills_22.parquet") %>%
  filter(bill_kind == "법률안", ppsr_kind == "의원",
         !grepl(",", rst_mona_cd))  # joint leads are comma-joined MONA_CDs

# Join ideal points to bills via the lead proposer's MONA_CD
bills_ip <- bills %>%
  inner_join(ip %>% filter(term == 22), by = c("rst_mona_cd" = "member_id"))

feols(enacted ~ ideal_point + I(ideal_point^2) | committee_nm, data = bills_ip)

As dependent variable

What predicts a legislator’s ideological position?

# Merge legislator metadata, one row per member of the 22nd
members <- read_parquet("data/processed/members_22.parquet") %>%
  transmute(member_id = mona_cd,
            pr_elected = election_type == "비례대표",
            female = sex == "여",
            seniority = term_number)  # seniority at the 22nd, not reelection

ip_full <- ip %>%
  filter(term == 22) %>%
  left_join(members, by = "member_id")

# Does electoral method matter? party_bloc, not party, as the fixed effect,
# because the proportional members of the two main blocs were elected on
# satellite lists (더불어민주연합, 국민의미래)
feols(ideal_point ~ pr_elected + female + seniority | party_bloc, data = ip_full)

Spatial voting models

For advanced applications, the raw roll-call data enables custom ideal point estimation:

library(tidyr)
library(pscl)
library(wnominate)

# Load roll calls (20th-22nd, one row per term, bill_id and member_id)
votes <- read_parquet("data/processed/roll_calls_all.parquet") %>%
  filter(term == 22)

# Pivot to vote matrix (legislators x votes), keyed on MONA_CD because
# names repeat. 찬성 = yea, 반대 = nay, 기권 and 불참 = missing, and no
# row = not seated at the time of the vote.
vote_matrix <- votes %>%
  mutate(vote_num = case_when(
    vote == "찬성" ~ 1, vote == "반대" ~ 6, TRUE ~ 9
  )) %>%
  select(member_id, bill_id, vote_num) %>%
  pivot_wider(names_from = bill_id, values_from = vote_num, values_fill = 0)

# Estimate with wnominate, with the kna thresholds (2.5% minority, 20
# votes) and the kna sign anchor 추경호 (G152611B) on both dimensions
rc <- rollcall(as.matrix(vote_matrix[, -1]),
               yea = 1, nay = 6, missing = 9, notInLegis = 0,
               legis.names = vote_matrix$member_id)
result <- wnominate(rc, dims = 2, minvotes = 20, lop = 0.025,
                    polarity = c("G152611B", "G152611B"))
